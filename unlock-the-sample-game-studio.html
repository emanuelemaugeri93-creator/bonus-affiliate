    Quick start

    • Step 1: upload an image, give the game a title and your name or brand.
    • Step 2: pick a game and a difficulty. Press Next.
    • Step 3: paste the link you want to unlock. Pick a look. Press Next.
    • Step 4: play your game in the preview, then press Export and upload the file to a free host.

    Step 1: your image

    What can I use?

    • A book cover for a free-chapter game.
    • A product photo for a coupon or a sample game.
    • A Pinterest pin or an ad image so people play before they click.
    • A logo, mascot or illustration for brand games.
    • A recipe or workbook page to give away a printable.

    Choosing a good image

    • Pick pictures with clear shapes and colors in every area. A flat white background makes puzzle pieces look identical and the game too hard.
    • Big images are shrunk to 900 px on the long side and saved as JPEG. The studio lowers the quality step by step so the finished file stays under about 700 KB.
    • Use only images you own or have the right to use.

    Crop

    "Keep the original shape" uses the whole image. The other choices cut from the center: square (best for Rotate Tiles), 4:5 and 2:3 (portrait, covers and pins), 3:2 and 16:9 (landscape). You can change the crop at any time.

    Title, name and theme

    The title and your name are shown above the game. The theme only changes which of the 20 hint templates are offered in Code Lock.

    Code Lock and the hint templates

    • Choose 4 digits or 4 letters and type your secret code.
    • Write 3 riddle hints. Players see hint 1 first and can reveal the next ones.
    • 20 templates are ready for you. Each one is built from your title and name (for example: "How many letters are in the first word of the title?") and shows the answer it gives. Pick a template for a hint, then set your code to match.
    • "Fill hints and code from templates" does everything for you: it fills character 1, character 2 and characters 3 and 4 (two clues in hint 3).
    • Hot means right, warm means near, cold means far. On Hard players only see how many are hot.

    Step 3: unlock settings

    • Unlock button text: what the button says, for example "Get my free chapter".
    • Unlock link: required. It must start with https:// or http://. Good ideas: a public PDF link, a landing page, a download page, a coupon page.
    • Heading: the line shown when the game is won. Leave it as it is or write your own.
    • Second button: optional, for a link you provide such as a signup page. The game never collects emails itself.
    • UTM: optional tags added to your links so your analytics shows where visitors came from. Example: source = pinterest, medium = game, campaign = spring. A link such as https://site.com/free becomes https://site.com/free?utm_source=pinterest&utm_medium=game&utm_campaign=spring.
    • Intro (140 characters) sits above the game. Outro sits in the unlock box.
    • Look: 6 ready looks, or "From my image" which picks readable colors from your picture. The studio checks the contrast.
    • High contrast: a black and white look. Players can switch it on inside the game too.

    Advanced features

    • Challenge stages (step 2): chain up to 3 games. Press "Add a stage", pick its game and options, repeat. Players finish stage 1, press "Start stage 2", and so on. The link unlocks after the last stage. Each stage has its own tab; the seed code stores only the stage on screen.
    • Templates (step 1): five ready-made setups (book, discount, printable, brain teaser, spot the changes). They set the game, texts, look and skip time. They never touch your image, title or link.
    • Custom colors and logo (step 3): choose "Custom colors" in the look list and pick your brand colors. The studio checks the contrast and warns you if text would be hard to read. A logo (PNG, JPG or WebP) is shrunk to fit and shown above the title.
    • Skip option: after the time you choose, a "Skip and get the link" button appears. Good for fairness and accessibility: nobody is locked out by a game they cannot finish.
    • Offer end date: after that day the game shows your message instead (for launches and limited offers). The date is checked on the player's device, so it is a convenience, not security.
    • Share my result: the unlock box offers a button that shares or copies a line such as "I finished My Game in 1:23!".
    • Play events: the game tells the page that embeds it when something happens (load, start, stage_complete, win, skip, unlock_click, share). The messages use postMessage and, if your page has Google Tag Manager, window.dataLayer. The game never sends data to a server.
    • Embed layout: a compact version that reports its height, for use inside an iframe on your own site. The Share kit in step 4 gives you the iframe code with the auto-resize script.
    • Share kit (step 4): paste the address of your hosted game and get ready-to-copy texts: the direct link, the embed code, a Pinterest description, an email and a social post.

    Step 4: play-test and export

    • The phone-shaped box runs the exact file you will export. Play it to the end to see the unlock box.
    • The file size is shown. Under 700 KB is ideal. If it is larger, use a smaller or simpler image.
    • Export downloads one .html file, named from your title.
    • Save project keeps your settings and the image in a .json file, so you can come back later. Load project opens it.
    • Your settings (not the image) are also saved automatically in this browser.
    • Undo and Redo are in the top bar. Shortcuts: Alt + Left or Right = previous or next step, Ctrl + Z and Ctrl + Y = undo and redo, Ctrl + E = export, ? = this guide.

    Hosting in 4 steps

    1. Export your game. You get a single file such as my-game-sample-game.html. It works offline and needs no other files.
    2. Upload it to a free host. Pick ONE:
      • GitHub Pages: create a free GitHub account and a new public repository. Press Add file, then Upload files, drop your game and rename it index.html if it is the only page. Press Commit. Open Settings, then Pages, choose the main branch and Save. After about a minute your address appears: https://yourname.github.io/repository-name/
      • Netlify Drop: make a folder, put your game in it and rename the file to index.html. Open app.netlify.com/drop and drag the folder onto the page. You get a link at once. Create a free account to keep it.
      • Your own website: upload the file with your host's File Manager or FTP, for example into a folder called games. The address is https://yoursite.com/games/my-game-sample-game.html. WordPress Media does not accept .html files, so use File Manager or FTP.
    3. Copy the address and open it in a normal browser tab. It should show your game.
    4. Test on your phone and win once. Check that the unlock button opens the right link. Then share it.

    Where to share the link

    • Your book or product page and the "about" page: "Play to unlock a free sample".
    • Pinterest pins: use the game address as the pin destination link. Add a line to the pin description such as "Play the puzzle to get the free sample".
    • Emails and newsletters, social bios and link-in-bio pages.
    • QR codes: paste the address into any free QR code maker and print it on a flyer or a bookmark.
    • Give each place its own UTM campaign tag so you can see which one works.

    Test checklist

    • Open the hosted address on your phone and on a computer.
    • Win the game once. Does the unlock box show? Does the button open the right page in a new tab?
    • Try the keyboard: Tab to the game and play with the arrow keys (slide, rotate) or type the code.
    • Switch on High contrast and check that you can still read everything.
    • If you changed the game later, export again and upload the new file over the old one.

    Troubleshooting

    • The image looks too easy or too hard. Pick a different game or difficulty, or crop to a more detailed part. Flat backgrounds make puzzle pieces look the same.
    • The file is over 700 KB. Use a smaller or less detailed image. Photos with lots of tiny noise take more space.
    • My link is refused. It must start with https:// or http://, with no spaces. javascript: and data: links are blocked on purpose.
    • The unlock button does nothing in the preview. Pop-ups open in a new tab. Allow pop-ups for the page, or test the exported file.
    • The jigsaw pieces are not visible. They wait in the tray below the board. Scroll down on a phone.
    • Drag feels stuck on a phone. Keep your finger on the piece. The page does not scroll while you drag a piece.
    • I lost my work. Use Load project if you saved one. The studio also remembers your settings in this browser, but not the image: upload it again.
    • I want a different game from the same image. Go back to step 2, pick another game and export again. The image stays.

    Good to know

    The unlock link is not secret. Anyone who has the game file can read the URL inside it. This is a fun gate, not security. Do not use it to protect paid content.
    • The exported game collects no data, sets no cookies and makes no network requests. It works offline.
    • All your text is shown as plain text, so special characters are safe.
    • The games can be played with a mouse, a finger or a keyboard. Screen readers hear moves and the win. Players who prefer less movement get no confetti and no shaking.
