How scoring works

Every category is a pure function of your text, scored from 0 to 100. Nothing is sent anywhere: it all runs in this page.

Weights

Overall = Hook 25% + Stakes 20% + Genre 20% + Clarity 15% + Tone 10% + Length 10% (total 100%).

Hook (100)

  • 30: first sentence length. Full marks at 8 to 22 words, fewer points the further outside that range.
  • 15: protagonist cue (a name or role) in the first 150 characters.
  • 20: threat cue in the first 150 characters.
  • 20: opens with a question or a stark, short statement.
  • 15: no cliché opener (20 openers such as "In a world where...").

Stakes (100)

  • Up to 60: consequence cues, 12 per distinct cue (or die, before, unless, if she fails, last chance...).
  • 20: a numbered deadline ("72 hours"); 12 for a softer time cue ("by midnight").
  • 20: a named cost (what the hero will lose or pay); 10 for a bare cost word.

Genre signals (100)

  • Up to 70: 7 per distinct urban fantasy term from a bank of 150+.
  • Up to 15: terms that fit your chosen subtype (5 each).
  • Up to 15: specific setting words such as precinct, alley, rooftop (5 each).
  • Penalty: 6 per term strongly tied to another subtype beyond the first 2 (max 24), and 10 per off-genre term such as "spaceship" (max 20).

Clarity (100)

  • 40: average sentence length, best at 12 to 20 words (4 points lost per word outside).
  • 35: Flesch reading ease estimate, best from 55 to 85.
  • 25: no more than 3 distinct capitalized names in the first 100 words (8 points lost per extra name).

Tone match (100)

  • 60: distinct words from the list for your chosen tone, 15 each up to 4.
  • 40: how close the balance of positive and negative words is to what the tone expects.

Length and structure (100)

  • 40: best at 150 to 300 words.
  • 15: tagline present.
  • 20: 3 to 5 short paragraphs.
  • 15: closing call to action.
  • 10: simple formatting only (no HTML or markdown clutter).

Honest limits: this is a heuristic scorer. It counts patterns. It cannot predict sales, rankings or how a reader will feel.

How to use Blurb Scorecard

Paste your book description and see how it scores before readers see it. It is a guide, not a verdict.

Step by step

  1. Paste your blurb on the left.Put the book description in the big box. You can add a one-line tagline above it. No blurb yet? Press Try a sample blurb to see how it works.
  2. Choose your genre subtype and tone.Pick the kind of urban fantasy you write (detective, noir, fae, witches, vampires, monster hunters) and the mood you want (dark noir, witty, thrilling, atmospheric). The score checks your words against these choices.
  3. Read the score on the right.The big number is the overall score. Below it are six category scores: Hook, Stakes, Genre signals, Clarity, Tone match, Length and structure. The radar chart shows them as one shape: the bigger and rounder, the better balanced.
  4. Open "Why this score".Under each bar, this button lists exactly what earned or lost points, so you know what to change instead of guessing.
  5. Check the preview and the underlined sentences.The highlighted part is roughly what readers see before "Read more" on a phone. Underlined sentences are too long or carry no story cue: hover (or Tab to) one to read why.
  6. Fix the top 5.The list ranks concrete actions by how many points they could add. Change one thing in the box on the left and the score updates by itself a moment later.
  7. Compare versions.Press Save as A (then B, C) before big changes. The right side then shows all versions side by side, in a table and on the radar chart. Load A brings a version back.
  8. Add your branding and export.Fill in Book title and Author, then use Copy report, Download report (.md) or Download score card (PNG) for a shareable picture.

Good to know

  • The scorer is a heuristic: it counts patterns in your text. It cannot predict sales or rankings, and a high score is not a promise of results.
  • Change one thing at a time. That way you can see which edit moved the score.
  • The open "How scoring works" panel on the left shows every formula in plain words.
  • Your draft, saved versions and names stay in this browser, so you can come back later.

If something looks wrong

  • No chart? If your connection blocks the chart library, the scores still appear as bars. Everything else works.
  • A message says there is nothing to export? The exports need some text in the blurb box first.
  • The score card PNG fonts look different? It is drawn with fallback fonts when the web fonts are not available. The numbers and layout are the same.
