How to use

Make short "can you solve it?" videos where a picture-logic puzzle fills itself in square by square, then reveals the clue. Post them on TikTok, Reels, Shorts or Pinterest to send readers to your puzzle book.

Quick start
  1. Pick a clue picture (or draw one, or turn an emoji into one).
  2. Write your hook, the case line and the reveal line. Use New case line for ideas.
  3. Choose a theme and format, then press Build my reel to watch it. Press Download video when you like it.
The inputs
  • Library: 15 detective pictures drawn square by square, each checked to have exactly one solution.
  • Draw your own: click or drag on the grid (10, 15 or 20 squares wide), then Use this drawing.
  • Emoji: paste any emoji; Silhouette fills its whole shape, Dark details keeps only its darker parts.
  • Image: upload a PNG or JPG. Best results: one clear subject on a plain background (icons, logos, clipart, silhouettes, an object on a white table). Auto finds the subject and zooms on it; try Dark parts, Outline or Invert if it looks off, move Sensitivity, pick 20 or 25 squares for more detail, then press Edit in Draw to fix single squares by hand. Busy photos, faces and landscapes can't keep their details in a small grid.
  • My pictures: press Save to my pictures in Draw, Emoji or Image to keep a picture in your own library (it stays on this device). Use Export to back it up or move it to another computer, and Import to load it back.
  • Solve check: the green light means the puzzle has one answer that can be found by logic alone. Yellow means a solver would need to guess: change a few squares.
  • Solve style: Detective fills squares in the order a real solver would find them (with X marks on empty squares); Rows, Spiral and Scatter are visual styles.
  • Pause and solve: adds a 3-2-1 countdown so viewers can pause and try first. That is what makes people comment and save.
Exports
  • Video: recorded in real time from the preview (MP4 in recent Chrome and Safari, otherwise WebM). Keep the tab in front while it records.
  • Cover frame: use it as the video thumbnail or a Pinterest pin.
  • Answer frame: post it the next day as "the answer".
  • Puzzle post: a square, printable challenge with clues only.
Tips
  • Post the reel, then the answer frame 24 hours later: two posts from one puzzle.
  • 9:16 for TikTok, Reels and Shorts; 4:5 for the Instagram feed; 1:1 for Facebook and Pinterest.
  • Keep the solve between 6 and 9 seconds: long enough to watch, short enough to replay.
Troubleshooting
  • Got a .webm file? TikTok and YouTube accept it. For Instagram, open the file in any free converter (or record in Safari / recent Chrome, which save MP4).
  • The video looks choppy? Close other heavy tabs while it records.
  • Shortcuts: Space play/pause, R rebuild, S surprise me.
