Guide: how this tool works

What you are making

An interactive story game (like a choose-your-own-adventure book) set in history. The player reads a scene, picks one of several choices, and the story goes on. Their choices change stats (such as Cash or Trust), what they carry, and which of several endings they reach. You build it here with no code, test it, and download one .html file to sell or share.

Three ways to start (pick one)

  1. Import from text with an AI (fastest, about 5 minutes). On the Story tab press Import from text. Press Copy AI prompt and paste it into your AI chat (ChatGPT, Claude...). Write your story idea where it says WRITE HERE (for example "a Venetian glassworks in 1510, the player is an apprentice who found a stolen recipe") and send. Paste the AI's answer into the box and press Check only to see problems, then Import. You get all the scenes, choices, stats, items and conditions at once.
  2. New story wizard (no AI needed). Press New story wizard, answer six questions (title, era, two stats, how many decision steps, how many endings, look). You get a working skeleton with an opening, branching steps, endings and a game over rule. Every scene contains [text in brackets] to replace with your own writing; the Test tab lists the ones you have not replaced yet.
  3. Start from a ready story. Six finished stories are included (use the box next to Load story). Press ▶ Play-test, play one for two minutes, then change its texts, names and endings. You can resell your edited version.

After you have a story: 4 steps

  1. Edit scenes. Each box on the Story map is a scene. Click one, then change its title and text in the panel on the right. The arrows between boxes are the choices.
  2. Add or link. + Scene adds a box. To link two scenes, drag from the round dot on the right of a scene onto another scene, then type the choice text.
  3. Mark the start and the endings. Exactly one scene is the Start. Scenes where the story stops are Endings (set Type in the panel). Every path should be able to reach an ending.
  4. Check, then export. The Test tab finds problems for you. The Export tab sets the title, theme and buttons, then press Download game.

Import from text: the format in 30 seconds

You rarely need to write it yourself, because the AI prompt contains the full rules. But it is simple and you can edit it by hand. The button Fill with my current story turns any story on the map into this text, so you can send it to an AI and ask for changes ("make the third scene scarier", "add two endings") and then import the result again.

Title: My story
Stat: Trust, 0 to 10, start 5, icon heart
Item: Old Key, icon key - Rusty, but it works.
Game over: if Trust <= 0 -> e_lost

## start | The Gate | start | mystery
Scene text goes here, on one or more lines.
> Pick up the key -> grass | do get Old Key
> Open the gate -> garden | if have Old Key
> Risky shortcut -> garden | chance 40% else grass

## e_lost | Lost | end-bad
The ending text.
  • ## id | Title | type | mood starts a scene. Types: start, normal, end-good, end-neutral, end-bad.
  • > choice text -> target is a choice (the target is a scene id or title). Add parts after |: if a condition, hide, do effects, chance 40% else another scene.
  • Conditions: Cash >= 3, have Key, not have Key, Seen is on. Effects: Cash -2, get Key, lose Key, flag Seen on. Avoid the words "and" and "or" inside names.

The four tabs

  • Story: the map of scenes and choices, and the panel where you edit them.
  • Stats: create the things the game keeps track of (see below) and "game over" rules.
  • Test: (1) plain-English problem list (including leftover [placeholder] text); (2) a path explorer that tries every combination to prove each ending can be reached and no player can get stuck; (3) a simulator that plays 1,000 random games and shows how often each ending happens.
  • Export: game title, theme, text speed, button link, then Play-test and downloads (game, project file, story outline, story map picture, share image).

Words you will see

  • Scene: one screen of story: a title, some text, an optional picture and choices.
  • Choice: a button that sends the player to another scene.
  • Ending: a scene that finishes the game. Mark it Good, Neutral or Bad. Players collect endings in a gallery.
  • Number (stat): a score with a minimum and maximum, shown as a bar. Examples: Cash 0-10, Reputation 0-10.
  • Flag: a yes/no fact, such as "You know the vase is a fake".
  • Item: something the player can carry, such as a Brass Key.
  • Effect: a change that happens. "On enter" effects happen when the player arrives in a scene; choice effects happen when it is picked. Examples: Cash decrease by 2, give Brass Key, switch a flag on.
  • Condition: a rule for a choice. Example: "Cash is at least 3" or "have the Brass Key". If it is not met the choice is shown locked (with the reason) or hidden, your pick.
  • Chance outcome: a choice that randomly leads to one of two scenes, for example 60% success, otherwise failure.
  • Game over rule (Stats tab): "if Cash is at most 0, jump to the Bankrupt ending", whatever scene the player is in.

Working on the map

  • Drag the background to move around. Scroll or pinch to zoom. Fit shows everything. Auto-layout tidies the scenes from left to right.
  • Click an arrow (or its label) to edit that choice. Drag the round end of a selected arrow onto another scene to point it somewhere else.
  • Hold Shift and drag to select several scenes. Ctrl/Cmd+C, V and D copy, paste and duplicate. Delete removes. Arrow keys nudge. Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes (50 steps).
  • Everything saves automatically in this browser. Use Save project to keep a file you can load later (pictures included).

How the player plays the game

They press New game, read the scene, and pick a choice by clicking, tapping, or pressing the number keys 1-9. Locked choices show what is needed. The stats bar and an Items button show their progress. The Menu button pauses and offers 3 save slots. When they reach an ending they see a summary, a "new ending discovered" banner, your button link, and can play again to find other endings.

Tips for a good story

  • Aim for 15-30 scenes, 3-5 endings, and 2-3 choices per scene. Keep scene text under 1,200 characters.
  • Let early choices matter later: give an item in one scene and require it in another, or change a stat that a final choice depends on.
  • Always give each scene at least one choice that has no condition, so nobody gets stuck. The Test tab warns you if they can.
  • Only upload pictures you made or are allowed to sell. Pictures are optional and are shrunk to 1,000 px.
